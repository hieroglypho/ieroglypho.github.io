Getting started

Placing glyphs on the canvas

  • Drag & drop any glyph from the character list on the right onto the canvas — it snaps to the nearest grid point.
  • Search by Gardiner code or name in the top field of the right panel.
  • Browse by Gardiner category from the or A, B, C.. dropdown.
  • The Gardiner: strip above the canvas lists the code of every sign in reading order (rows top to bottom, as →MdC reads them). Selected signs light up there; click a code to select its sign, Shift-click to add it to the selection. Hide the strip with its × or from the ≡ menu (Hide / Show Gardiner codes).

Pasting a series (MdC)

  • The small Paste series box (lower-left, above the toolbar) accepts either:
    • Gardiner codes separated by -, space or comma — e.g. A1-D21-N35.
    • A run of raw hieroglyph characters — e.g. 𓂀𓏏𓊵 — pasted from any text.
    • MdC sound letters, where case matters as in JSesh: A = 𓄿 ꜣ, a = 𓂝 ꜥ; h = 𓉔, H = 𓎛 ḥ; x = 𓐍 ḫ, X = 𓄡 ẖ; s = 𓋴, S = 𓈙 š; t = 𓏏, T = 𓍿 ṯ; d = 𓂧, D = 𓆓 ḏ; also i y w b p f m n r l z q k g. Gardiner codes ignore case (aa1 = Aa1).
    • The longer MdC sound codes, as in JSesh: ra = 𓇳, nTr = 𓊹, anx = 𓋹, Hm = 𓍛 …, and JSesh's codes for variant signs (M1A, V23A), which also reach the Extended-A signs.
    • A mix of these.
  • Press Enter or click Add Glyphs to drop the whole sequence onto the canvas in order.

Linear vs. spatial

  • Choose Individual signs in the dialog to lay glyphs out in 2D the way textbooks and JSesh do.
  • Single text run stays linear and can't show stacking — but spatial operators auto-switch to the layout engine, so you rarely have to think about it.
  • Tick ← Right to left to write the other way: every sign faces right, rows start at the right edge, and cartouche ties and brackets turn round too. Signs marked mirrored with \ face left. (Right to left always uses individual signs.)
  • See MdC spatial layout for the full operator and markup reference.

MdC spatial layout — operators & markup

Manuel de Codage notation typed into the Individual signs input. Operators below run tightest → loosest; everything composes, so a span can be coloured, enclosed, and bracketed at once. Each symbol is shown with its meaning and a worked example.

Round-trip: the reverse also works — select signs on the canvas and press M (or the →MdC toolbar button) to copy them back out as MdC code, with the * : - structure and any line breaks reconstructed from their layout.

Editing & shortcuts

Selecting and moving objects

  • Select: click a glyph or shape. Drag to move; drag a corner to scale; drag the top handle to rotate.
  • Click the ink, not the box: selection hit-tests against the sign's actual pixels, so when two glyphs sit close together you grab the one your cursor is on — no more fighting their oversized bounding boxes.
  • Multi-select: Shift + click, or marquee-drag an empty area. Ctrl + A selects everything on the canvas; Esc deselects.
  • Copy / paste: Ctrl + C then Ctrl + V stamps a copy with a small offset (repeat to cascade); Ctrl + J duplicates in place; Ctrl + X cuts.
  • Fine move: select, then use arrow keys (hold Shift for 1px steps).
  • Edit text: double-click a text object.
  • Pan canvas: hold Alt and drag.
  • Zoom: mouse wheel over the canvas.

Custom text and transliteration

  • Click ⌨ to open the text dialog.
  • The dialog includes special-character keys for Egyptological transliteration (ꜣ ꜥ ḥ ḫ ẖ š ḳ ṯ ḏ) and a Coptic row.
  • Set the font size before inserting, or double-click the text on the canvas to edit it later.

Dictionary search

  • The dictionary is built in and loads automatically — no upload needed.
  • Search by Gardiner codes separated by spaces (e.g. A1 Z2) or by transliteration (e.g. rHty).
  • Matches are highlighted; non-ASCII text is enlarged for readability.
  • TLA entries: entries from the Thesaurus Linguae Aegyptiae end with their lemma number (e.g. TLA 10030). Click it to open that lemma's page on the TLA site, with its spellings, attestations and bibliography. It also links from “What does this say?” results.
  • Resize results: drag the small horizontal handle just below the character list to grow or shrink the results panel.
  • Drag to canvas: highlight glyphs in a result (e.g. 𓀀𓁐𓏥) and drag the selection onto the canvas — they're placed as a bottom-aligned row.

“What does this say?” — read glyphs back

  • Right-click a sign on the canvas and choose 𓂀 What does this say? to look it up. Right-click a selection of signs to read them together, or right-click empty space to read the whole canvas.
  • Signs are read in reading order — rows top-to-bottom, left-to-right within a row, top-to-bottom within a stacked cadrat — not as an unordered jumble.
  • Results are ranked by closeness: Exact (the signs are a word), then words containing them (your signs are part of a longer word), then words found inside a longer run, then single-sign readings (collapsed).
  • Click any glyph run in the results to drop those signs back onto the canvas.

𓏞 Inspect / Convert — every form of the selection

  • Select signs on the canvas, then right-click and choose 𓏞 Inspect / Convert. A small panel shows the selection in five forms, each with a 📋 copy button:
  • Glyphs — the signs themselves in reading order.
  • Unicode with layout — the signs as Unicode text that keeps the stacking, previewed as it will look (see below).
  • Code points — e.g. U+131CB U+13429.
  • MdC — Manuel de Codage, the same string the →MdC button copies.
  • Reading — judged on all the selected signs together. If they spell a word in the dictionary, that reading is shown. Otherwise you get a per-sign breakdown (· separates signs, ? marks a sign with no entry) and a note that the combination may still be a valid word that simply isn't in the dictionary yet.

Saving & exporting

File menu (≡) — save, load, export, wiki

The ≡ button on the right side of the toolbar opens a single menu:

  • Save as JSON: full workspace snapshot (canvas + Gardiner strip).
  • Save as PDF: exports a US-Letter page with the canvas as a picture, plus hidden text over it, so you can select, copy and search the signs, transliteration and translation in any PDF reader. The signs are written in reading order, the same order as Copy as Unicode, even after you move signs around by hand. With the Page guide on, the PDF is exactly the page you see — glyphs keep their positions at 1:1 (turn the guide on, lay out within it, then export). With the guide off, the export takes in all your work (also any part beyond the visible canvas), centres it, and scales down only if it is larger than the page (you'll see a "scaled to N%" note). A long text goes over several pages instead of shrinking: the PDF breaks between rows, never through a sign, and numbers the pages at the foot. The PDF library loads on first click.
  • Save as PDF (vector text): the same page, but every sign and every word is real text in an embedded font, and lines and frames are vector shapes. It stays sharp at any zoom or print size, and the file is much smaller. The signs are in reading order here too. The fonts are Noto Sans Egyptian Hieroglyphs, Hieroglyphica Gardiner Light and Hieroglyphica Magna for signs, Gentium Plus for serif text such as transliteration (the PDF shows proper ꜣ and ꜥ), and Noto Sans for other text; the PDF holds only the characters you used. A character none of these fonts has (Coptic, emoji) is drawn as a small picture in its place.
  • Open JSON…: load a previously saved workspace.
  • Open JSesh file (.gly)…: lay a text written in JSesh out on the canvas, below anything already there (or drop the .gly file on the canvas). Stacks, ligatures, cartouches, colour, shading, brackets, mirrored, turned and resized signs come across; the file's title and notes become a text block above the signs. A text written right to left opens right to left; a text written in columns is laid out in rows (right to left if the file says so). Signs with no Unicode character (JSesh's own additions) show as a dashed box, and a note lists their codes. Ctrl+Z takes the whole text back off. Sign codes come from Unicode's sign database (Unikemet) and JSesh's Manuel de Codage table (Serge Rosmorduc, public domain).
  • Gardiner signs (Wiki): opens the Wikipedia sign list in a new tab.
  • Right-click the canvas for the editor's own menu: look up or inspect signs, copy as Unicode, copy the image, and the same Save as PNG / SVG / PDF exports.

Copy as Unicode text (with layout)

  • Select signs (or nothing, for the whole canvas), then press U or right-click → 𓐰 Copy as Unicode (with layout). You get real text you can paste into a document, a web page, an email or a database.
  • The stacking is kept with Unicode's hieroglyph layout characters (U+13430 onward): one sign over another, side by side inside a group, and brackets for groups inside groups. Lines stay lines.
  • Cartouches and brackets are kept too, in →MdC as well: a cartouche, serekh, ḥwt or fortified wall is written with Unicode's enclosure characters (in MdC <-…->, <S-…->S and so on), the editorial brackets with Unicode's text-critical marks ⟦ ⟧ { } [ ] ⸢ ⸣ ⟨ ⟩ (in MdC [[ ]], [{ }], [" "], [' '], [& &]). A cartouche drawn with the cartouche tool counts when the signs sit inside it.
  • Fonts: the text shows stacked only in apps using a font that supports these characters, such as the free Egyptian Text (install it on your computer). With other fonts the signs appear in a row, some with small boxes between them; the text itself is still correct. When pasted into a word processor, the text arrives already set in Egyptian Text.
  • Reading order: when most of the signs face right, the text is read right to left, so the copy starts with the rightmost sign; a sign facing the other way gets Unicode's mirror mark. →MdC copies in the same reading order. Unicode leaves the direction to the app showing the text, so most apps will show it left to right.
  • Check first: right-click → 𓏞 Inspect / Convert shows the result in that font. The editor works out the groups from where the signs sit, so very tight or overlapping placements may group differently than you meant; nudge a sign and look again.
  • Pasting it back: text with these layout characters can be pasted into the 𓂀… dialog and is laid out again. Cartouches, the other enclosures and the text-critical marks are laid out again too; insertions (a small sign tucked into a corner) are placed side by side, and mirroring marks are dropped.
  • Copy image now also puts an SVG (vector) copy on the clipboard in browsers that allow it, so vector apps get a sharp image; other apps keep pasting the PNG.
Tip — workflow: build the line of glyphs first, then use H or T to snap them to a shared baseline, and Ctrl+D to distribute them evenly.
Tip — laying out a page: turn on the Page guide and arrange your glyphs inside the frame. The PDF then comes out exactly as shown — the solid line is the paper edge, the dotted line is the printable margin. Keep signs inside the dotted line and they print 1:1 in place; anything outside the solid edge is off the page. The guide itself is locked — it can't be selected, moved, or deleted, and it never appears in the export.

Tracing a photo

  • BG loads a photo behind the canvas (a stela, a papyrus, a tomb wall). Once a photo is loaded, BG opens the Photo tools.
  • Place it: drag the photo and its corner handles to move and size it, or use ⤢ Fit to view.
  • Straighten it: 📐 Straighten…, then click two points along a line that should be level or upright, such as a register line or the edge of a column. The photo turns so that line lies on the grid, without moving the part you clicked. For fine turns use the slider or − / + (half a degree); ⟲ 90° / ⟳ 90° fix a sideways photo; ↺ 0° undoes all turning. Ctrl+Z undoes each step.
  • Lock it: 🔒 Lock makes the photo ignore the mouse, so you can place, move and drag-select signs on top of it without grabbing the photo. Unlock to move it again.
  • BG α sets its opacity; 👁 Hide hides it to check your tracing.
  • Exports: untick Include photo… to save PNG, PDF, SVG and copied images with your signs only. A hidden photo is never exported. Share links never carry the photo; Save workspace files always do.
  • Replace… loads another photo in the same place, size and angle; Remove takes it away (Ctrl+Z brings it back).

Fonts

Signs are drawn with Noto Sans Egyptian Hieroglyphs (SIL Open Font License). The signs of Unicode's Extended-A block come from NewGardiner by Mark-Jan Nederhof, University of St Andrews (SIL Open Font License), and, for the signs NewGardiner leaves out, from Hieroglyphica Magna, drawn for this editor. Both are used here with lighter strokes, to match Noto; the lighter NewGardiner is renamed Hieroglyphica Gardiner Light, as its licence asks.
